# Model card: irrigation/persistence

## Model details

- **Track / deliverable:** Irrigation forecasting (D2)
- **Architecture:** Naive persistence: every 6/12/24/48 h forecast equals the latest valid soil-moisture reading; a fixed threshold recommends irrigation below 25.0.
- **Version / run:** Decision evidence is reproduced from YAML configs and DVC snapshots; historical exploratory runs also live in MLflow experiment `d2_irrigation`.
- **Config:** `configs/d2_irrigation/naive.yaml`; local serving config `configs/d6_serving/irrigation.yaml`
- **Author & date:** Sohan Shingade, 2026-08-05

## Intended use

- **Primary use:** Forecast near-term soil moisture and screen for irrigation need at instrumented Iron Horse Vineyard blocks.
- **Users:** Vineyard operators through the VINE API/dashboard, with grower review.
- **Out of scope:** Autonomous irrigation control; extrapolation to uninstrumented blocks or other vineyards; overriding grower judgment during frost, maintenance, sensor faults, or unusual field conditions.

## Training data

Persistence has no learned parameters. Evaluation used hourly sensor snapshots from five soil probes at Iron Horse Vineyard, covering approximately 2026-01-22 through 2026-07-08. The fifth probe (`SE0X-LS-1`) uses its first-depth raw LoRa soil channels after schema normalization.

Forecasts were evaluated at 6, 12, 24, and 48 hours. Missing readings and shared source outages were kept as gaps and excluded from scoring; they were never imputed. Weather was not required by persistence.

## Evaluation

- **Metrics:** Soil-moisture MAE/RMSE and irrigation-threshold precision/recall.
- **Protocol:** Expanding-window walk-forward evaluation on the chronological holdout half. Target-time-aligned learned models now purge the final `h-1` training labels at horizon `h`; persistence itself is a causal shift.
- **Baselines and challengers:** Persistence was the floor and remained the champion after per-sensor ridge, ridge with perfect-forecast features, ridge-delta, ARIMA, drydown trend, random forest, gradient boosting, pooled cross-sensor models, a water-balance weather-correction experiment, a Prophet seasonal forecaster with a soil-temperature regressor, an LSTM encoder-decoder sequence model, a diurnal-drift baseline that adds the expected cumulative hour-of-day change estimated in delta space, a cross-sensor error-correction model that forecasts each probe's change from its spread against the other probes' standardized levels, a probabilistic CRPS challenger that wraps the persistence center in a calibrated adaptive spread, and a rain-gated hybrid that applies the water-balance correction only when the forecast at the origin predicts material rain. Fifteen challenger families in total have been evaluated and rejected.
- **Observed performance:** On sensors whose holdouts cross the 25.0 threshold, persistence alert precision/recall is approximately 0.95 to 0.99. MAE rises with horizon: roughly 0.06 to 0.13 at 6 h, and 0.29 to 0.52 at 48 h across the five probes.
- **Water-balance status:** Research, not promoted. It has now been evaluated on real archived forecast vintages as well as the realized-weather oracle. The 48 h aggregate edge survives the switch (+5.3% to +13.5% across five probes, against +3.5% to +11.2% under the oracle), but 24 h skill flips negative on every probe (−2.8% to −8.1%), and worst-fold skill is negative in every cell where the correction is ever active (four 6/12 h cells sit at exactly 0.000 because the correction never fires there). The promotion gate therefore fails. See the [vintage validation report](../../reports/2026-08-04-d2-vintage-validation.md).
- **Prophet and LSTM status:** Research, not promoted. These complete the proposal's named forecaster list under the same walk-forward protocol and `h-1` label purge, evaluated on SE01-LS-1 at 6, 12, 24, and 48 h. Prophet (daily and weekly seasonality, decision-time soil temperature as an external regressor, one fit per fold) posts aggregate skill vs persistence of −14.705 at 6 h, −8.380 at 12 h, −4.882 at 24 h, and −2.474 at 48 h, with worst-fold skill between −28.796 and −14.471. The LSTM encoder-decoder (72 h input window, hidden 128, 2 layers, trained 20 epochs on CPU, reduced from the sketch's 50 for runtime) posts −4.240 at 6 h, −6.034 at 12 h, −6.160 at 24 h, and −8.255 at 48 h, with worst-fold skill between −131.310 and −15.779; its 72 h window also shrinks the shared scorable set (n 998 to 956, against 1367 to 1335 for the classical runs) because windows crossing sensor gaps are never imputed. Neither model beats persistence at any horizon, so the ADR-0003 worst-fold gate is never reached. Full tables: `docs/reports/assets/d5_prophet_results.csv` and `docs/reports/assets/d5_lstm_results.csv`.
- **Diurnal-drift status:** Research, not promoted. The twelfth challenger family tests the returns-seasonality hypothesis: soil moisture is near a random walk in levels while its hourly changes may follow the daily evapotranspiration cycle, so the forecast is persistence plus the expected cumulative hour-of-day change, estimated in delta space on the training fold only from gap-free consecutive-hour pairs, evaluated on all five probes at 6, 12, 24, and 48 h under the same walk-forward protocol and `h-1` label purge. The targeted short horizons fail: at 6 and 12 h aggregate skill vs persistence is positive only on SE01-LS-1 (+0.055 and +0.084) and negative on the other four probes (down to −3.999 and −3.178 on SE0X-LS-1), with negative worst-fold skill on every probe. At 24 and 48 h the hour-of-day structure nets out and the residual mean daily drift acts as a drydown correction, reaching aggregate skill +0.268 and +0.369 on SE01-LS-4 with positive worst-fold skill there and on SE01-LS-2 (+0.080 to +0.196), yet SE0X-LS-1 stays negative at every horizon, so no horizon passes the ADR-0003 worst-fold gate on all probes. A second variant conditioning the change table on the decision-time soil temperature tercile posts aggregate skill from −0.967 to +0.325 and has a positive worst fold in exactly one of twenty probe-horizon cells (SE01-LS-1 at 48 h, +0.017). Full table: `docs/reports/assets/d5_diurnal_results.csv`.
- **Error-correction status:** Research, not promoted. The thirteenth challenger family imports the pairs-trading premise: the five probes share one weather forcing, so their standardized levels may be cointegrated, and a probe that diverges from the cross-section should revert toward it. The forecast is the last observed level plus a closed-form OLS error-correction term on the decision-time spread, defined as the probe's z-score minus the mean of the other probes' z-scores with standardization constants from the training fold only, evaluated on all five probes at 6, 12, 24, and 48 h under the same walk-forward protocol and `h-1` label purge, on rows where the full cross-section is observed (n 1401 at 6 h to 1359 at 48 h). The premise holds only partially in sample: the spread's hourly AR(1) slope is 0.952 to 0.978 across every probe-horizon fold, so it does mean-revert, with an implied divergence half-life of roughly 14 to 31 hours, and the fitted beta is negative in 81 of 100 probe-horizon folds; SE01-LS-3 is the exception, with positive beta in 19 of its 20 folds, meaning its divergences tended to widen over the training span. The skill numbers reject the family. Aggregate skill vs persistence is negative in 19 of 20 probe-horizon cells; the sole positive cell is SE01-LS-2 at 24 h at +0.012 with worst fold −0.061. The other SE01 cells span −0.069 to −5.459, worst on SE01-LS-4, and SE0X-LS-1 posts −13.189 to −36.054 because it reads far drier than the fleet and the correction keeps pulling its forecast toward the cross-section. Worst-fold skill is negative in every cell (best −0.061 on SE01-LS-2 at 24 h, worst −83.676 on SE0X-LS-1 at 12 h), so the ADR-0003 gate fails everywhere. Full table: `docs/reports/assets/d5_ecm_results.csv`.
- **Probabilistic CRPS status:** Research, not promoted, and the first family to beat persistence in every cell on an aggregate metric. The fourteenth challenger changes the scoring rule rather than the forecast center: a deterministic forecast is a point-mass distribution whose CRPS equals its absolute error, so persistence's CRPS baseline equals its MAE, and a distribution with the same center and an honestly calibrated spread scores strictly better if the calibration is real. The model is a Gaussian centered on persistence with a causal EWMA estimate of the h-step error scale (halflife 72 pairs, warmup 24), evaluated on all five probes at 6, 12, 24, and 48 h under the same walk-forward protocol and `h-1` label purge. Aggregate CRPS skill vs the persistence point mass is positive in all 20 probe-horizon cells (+0.094 to +0.266), and 90 percent intervals cover 87 to 96 percent of actuals; 50 percent intervals over-cover (55 to 71 percent), so the true error distribution is more peaked than Gaussian. Two controls localize the value: a fixed per-fold spread has a positive worst fold in only 1 of 20 cells, and a climatology ensemble posts skill of −45.7 to −3.7, so the adaptive spread is what works. The worst-fold gate still fails in 3 of 20 cells: one is a 145 hour fold truncated by the shared May outage and the other two sit at essentially zero. Persistence stays served; this is the starting recipe if a probabilistic D6 product is ever wanted. Full table: `docs/reports/assets/d2_crps_results.csv`; see the [CRPS report](../../reports/2026-08-05-crps-probabilistic.md).
- **Rain-gated hybrid status:** Research, not promoted. The fifteenth challenger acts on the event study directly: predict persistence by default and switch to the vintage-forecast water balance only when forecast precipitation over the horizon window reaches a threshold chosen causally on training folds (84 of 100 fold selections picked 5.0 mm). The gate keeps essentially all of the water balance's event-window win (87 to 105 percent retention at 24 h, over 99 percent at 48 h; event-hour skill +0.026 to +0.625) and at 48 h the fired subset is positive on all five probes (+0.140 to +0.484), lifting aggregate 48 h skill to +0.051 to +0.122. Weak dominance still fails: at 24 h the fired subset itself is negative on all five probes (−0.094 to −0.267), and threshold choice barely matters (spread at most 0.005 across 0.5 to 5.0 mm) because the losses sit on forecast-rain windows that any precipitation gate also fires on, meaning forecast busts and drizzle rather than quiet hours. Worst-fold skill is negative in 15 of 20 cells and exactly zero in the rest, where the hybrid degenerates to persistence. Persistence stays served; the open question is forecast-bust robustness at 24 h, and the 48 h behavior is worth revisiting after a second wet season. Full table: `docs/reports/assets/d2_gated_results.csv`; see the [gated water balance report](../../reports/2026-08-05-gated-water-balance.md).
- **Event-conditioned check:** Scoring only the hours inside a detected rise event plus a trailing 24 h changes the picture of where errors live: on the four SE01 probes those hours are 2.2 to 4.6 percent of the scored holdout yet carry 17.3 to 39.8 percent of persistence's total absolute error. On event hours the vintage-forecast water balance beats persistence at 24 h (skill +0.030 to +0.283) and at 48 h (+0.224 to +0.626) on every probe with event evidence, while losing the quiet subset at 24 h (−0.104 to −0.216); diurnal drift stays within −0.073 to +0.061 on event hours. Every scored event hour comes from the single April 20 to 22 storm and SE0X-LS-1 contributes none, so the ship decision is unchanged and persistence stays served. See the [event study report](../../reports/2026-08-05-event-study.md); full table `docs/reports/assets/d2_event_study_results.csv`.
- **Literature position:** The shipped policy is not a fallback. Persistence plus a measured-state threshold is event-triggered (Lebesgue) control with observation-based triggering on a slow, directly measured, first-order plant, which is the structure the control literature derives as optimal or near-optimal for this system class (Åström & Bernhardsson 2002; Lipsa & Martins 2011; Soleymani et al. 2023). It is also the structure California vineyard practice converged on independently, where a soil-moisture depletion trigger is the standard rule and the water-balance ledger is the FAO-56 checkbook method. The best field-validated model predictive controller in the literature beats soil-water-sensor control by roughly 5 percent water at comparable yield, so the margins available above this baseline are small. See the [irrigation control policy review](../../reports/2026-08-05-irrigation-control-review.md).

## Limitations & caveats

- Evaluation covers one vineyard and about 5.5 months, not multiple full seasons.
- Persistence cannot anticipate rain or irrigation events; it follows the latest state.
- The 25.0 threshold is an experimental decision rule and needs operator confirmation/calibration by block and sensor depth.
- Simultaneous source gaps affected all devices, including a long 2026-06-24 to 2026-07-08 outage.
- A stale reading can produce a numerically valid persistence forecast that is operationally unsafe. D6 therefore exposes freshness and suppresses recommendations when stale.
- Water balance has been tested on both realized future weather and archived forecast vintages; under real forecasts it loses to persistence at 24 h and fails the worst-fold gate wherever the correction is active. The remaining open question is per-fold robustness to forecast busts, not a new model family.

## Ethical & operational considerations

False negatives can contribute to crop stress; false positives waste water. Recommendations must remain human-in-the-loop. Operators should inspect sensor freshness, field conditions, weather forecasts, and irrigation records before acting. The service must not silently recommend from stale, missing, or invalid data.
